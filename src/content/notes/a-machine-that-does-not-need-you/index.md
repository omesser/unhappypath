title: 'The real achievement is a machine that does not need you'

I'm back to work — well, for a day, kinda — from about a month of active reserve
service, trying to catch up on what's been happening. Checking in on the team
and the projects in flight. And you know what the best part is?

They carried on just fine without me.

Developers, architects, data engineers, data-ops. Cloud infra stayed stable.
Production is fine. Pipelines are flowing. Releases are happening. Data is being
annotated. Things are shipping. Wartime and all.

It's not as if there are zero issues. It's a hectic startup with all the
uncertainty that comes with that. Still: no drama, no frantic pings. Things are
not on fire.

Solid work from people who know their stuff, and how to keep the spice flowing.

The real achievement in management or leadership was always building a machine
that doesn't need you to babysit it. For now it's nice to check in every few
days, see that everyone is safe.

Sarcasm levels were getting dangerously low out there. So there's that.
